A galaxy divided: Star Wars factions and US partisan identity

How Americans project their politics onto the Rebel Alliance and Galactic Empire — and why both sides claim the rebels.

Key findings

What the research actually shows about Star Wars and American politics

01

Both sides claim the Rebels

Every major US political group identifies with the Rebel Alliance. The Empire, by contrast, has almost no admirers on the left — Empire identification is almost exclusively a right-wing phenomenon.

02

The backlash was mostly bots

Over 50% of the loudest Star Wars critics online were bots, trolls, or coordinated far-right accounts. The real fan dissatisfaction was much smaller than headlines suggested.

03

Andor radicalized the allegory

Earlier Star Wars was a vague anti-authoritarian myth anyone could claim. Andor (2022–25) made it explicit: Mon Mothma = institutional liberals, Saw Gerrera = revolutionary left, the Empire = modern fascist bureaucracy.

04

The Rorschach franchise

Republicans used Rebel imagery in 2006 campaign ads. Tea Partiers compared Obama to Palpatine. Then after 2016, liberals reclaimed the same symbols. Star Wars is the rare franchise both sides genuinely believe is about them.

The Rorschach asymmetry: Both parties have claimed the Rebel narrative — Republicans in 2006, Tea Partiers against Obamacare, Trump supporters against "the establishment." But only the right has developed a serious intellectual tradition of defending the Empire: the Weekly Standard's "Case for the Empire" (2002), William Kristol's "rooting for the Empire from the first moment," the online right's embrace of Imperial aesthetics. There is no liberal equivalent. This asymmetry is the key finding: Rebel identification is bipartisan, but Empire identification has a distinctly right-wing ideological home.
